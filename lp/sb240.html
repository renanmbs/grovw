Utah SB 240 · First-Time Homebuyer Assistance

Utah will put $20,000 toward your first home

Zero percent interest. No monthly payment on it. You repay it when you sell or refinance — not before. It applies to newly built homes under $450,000, and most people who qualify have never heard of it.

What the program actually is

SB 240 is a state-funded loan that sits quietly behind your main mortgage. It is not a grant, and it is not forgiven — but you make no payments on it while you live there.

Monthly payment on it

To qualify you need to

  • Be buying newly built, never-occupied construction
  • Keep the purchase price at or under $450,000
  • Have lived in Utah for at least 12 months
  • Not have owned a home in the past 3 years
  • Work through a participating lender

When you repay it

At sale or refinance, you repay the lesser of $20,000 or 50% of your home's equity. If your home hasn't gained value, you owe less than the full amount.

Funding is limited and first-come, first-served. The program is capped at roughly 2,400–2,500 buyers statewide, and more than 2,900 households have already been funded across earlier rounds. Availability changes — we confirm the current funding status with a participating lender before you rely on it.

The part most people miss

The new-construction requirement isn't a limitation — it's the whole strategy.

Because SB 240 only applies to newly built homes, the program effectively points you at builder inventory. Builders with homes sitting under the $450,000 cap are frequently willing to contribute toward closing costs or rate buy-downs on top of the state's $20,000, because a finished, unsold home costs them money every month.

That's where this stops being one program and starts being a stack. We know which builders across Davis, Weber, and Salt Lake counties currently have inventory under the cap, and which of them are still negotiating.

What can stack on top

  • Utah Housing Corporation FirstHome — down payment assistance up to 6% of the first mortgage, capped at $27,500
  • FHA financing — as little as 3.5% down with a 580+ credit score
  • VA financing — zero down, no mortgage insurance, if you've served
  • Builder incentives — closing cost credits and rate buy-downs on standing inventory
  • County programs — Weber, Davis, and Salt Lake each run their own, with separate rules

Not every combination is permitted together, and the rules change by lender. That's the conversation worth having before you tour a single home.
