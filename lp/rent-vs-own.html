Assumes a 30-year fixed loan. It excludes HOA dues, maintenance, and closing costs. Rates change daily and vary by credit profile — this is an estimate for planning, not a quote or a loan offer.

The down payment is usually the real obstacle — not the monthly payment. That's the specific thing Utah's assistance programs exist to solve. SB 240 offers up to $20,000 toward a newly built home under $450,000. Utah Housing FirstHome adds up to 6% of the loan, capped at $27,500. Weber, Davis, and Salt Lake counties each run their own programs on top of that.

What the calculator can't show you

At today's rates the monthly gap is usually real and usually uncomfortable. These are the things that sit outside the arithmetic and still matter.

  • Your rent will keep rising. A fixed mortgage payment doesn't — only the tax and insurance portions move, and they move slowly. The gap you see today is the widest it will ever be.
  • Principal is not an expense. A meaningful share of every payment goes to a balance you own rather than to a landlord.
  • Assistance changes the math entirely. If $20,000 of your down payment comes from the state at 0% interest, the cash-needed figure above may be badly overstated for you.
  • Maintenance is real. Owners should plan roughly 1% of the home's value annually. The calculator deliberately leaves it out so you can weigh it yourself.
