# Utah Down Payment Assistance & First-Time-Buyer Programs

Confidence legend: 🟢 primary/strong sourcing · 🟡 aggregator sourcing, verify · 🔴 pending/unconfirmed

## State programs

### SB 240 — First-Time Homebuyer Assistance 🟢
- **Up to $20,000, 0% interest, deferred** (no monthly payment)
- **New construction only**, never occupied, purchase price **≤ $450,000**
- Utah resident 12 months; no ownership in past 3 years
- Capped ~2,400–2,500 buyers, first-come first-served via participating lenders
- Repayment at sale/refi: lesser of $20,000 or 50% of home equity
- **Performance (Utah Senate data via Building Salt Lake, as of Jan 30, 2026):**
  2,934 households funded, avg loan $19,948, **>$58.5M distributed**, avg home price
  ~$388,698, avg awardee household income ~$90,321
- Still active 2026; Legislature added **~$10M** this session (per KSL session wrap-up)
- Senate President Adams: **no eligibility/terms changes**, new-build requirement stays
- 🔴 **HB 541** (Rep. Verona Mauga) proposed extending to existing homes up to $10,000 —
  introduced, **not confirmed passed.** Do not advertise until confirmed.

**Ad angle:** strongest hook for new-build communities (Daybreak-type). The $450K cap and
new-construction rule are natural qualifiers that self-select the audience — exactly what
you need under HEC.

### Utah Housing Corporation (UHC) 🟢
- Active products: **FirstHome, FHA/VA, Freddie Mac HFA Advantage, Score, NoMI**
- ⚠️ **HomeAgain is temporarily suspended** — route repeat buyers to FHA/VA or HFA Advantage
- **DPA up to 6% of first mortgage, capped at $27,500**, in two structures:
  - **Traditional second:** 1 percentage point above your UHC first rate (ceiling 8%)
  - **Deferred second:** 3.5% accrued interest, no monthly payment
- FirstHome: ~660 credit score for conventional; **homebuyer education required**

**2026 purchase-price limits by county** 🟡 *(mortgage-info.com, updated May 2026)*
| County | Limit |
|---|---|
| Salt Lake | $562,000 |
| Davis | $555,000 |
| Weber | $495,000 |
| Utah | $542,000 |
| Summit / Park City (high cost) | $650,000 |

Income limits vary by county/household — Salt Lake County family of two ≈ $110,700 🟡
(aggregator figure; confirm on UHC's limits page).

## County & city programs

### Own in Ogden (Weber) 🟡
Ogden city limits, 0% deferred, repaid at sale/refi, **first-time status not required**:
- **$10,000** general
- **$15,000** state-certified K-12 teachers & Ogden City employees
- **$20,000** sworn Ogden police & firefighters

Price cap ~$430,000 existing / ~$467,000 new.

**Ad angle:** the occupation tiers are outstanding creative hooks. Naming "Ogden teachers"
or "Ogden police & fire" in the ad copy is **not prohibited targeting** — you are describing
the program, not filtering the audience. This is the cleanest way to get precision under HEC.

### Weber County grant 🟡
~**$5,000**, secured as a lien, **forgiven after 5 years** (CDBG-funded). Federal
HOME-funded deferred loans also available in the Ogden area.

### Davis County Homeownership Assistance 🟡
- **1% loan up to $50,000**, deferred, repaid at sale/refi
- ≤80% AMI; **not limited to first-time buyers**
- ⚠️ **2025 funds exhausted; applications scheduled to reopen ~July 1, 2026**
- **Confirm before advertising** — largest dollar figure available, so highest temptation
  and highest false-advertising risk if stale.

### Salt Lake area 🟡
- **Salt Lake City HOME:** ~$10,000
- **CDCU** (Salt Lake City / County / Taylorsville): deferred forgivable loan
- **NeighborWorks Salt Lake:** ~$10,000 forgivable
- **West Valley City:** forgivable loans up to ~$14,999

### FHLB HELP 🟡
~**$20,000 forgivable grant**, 5-year owner-occupancy requirement, available across
Weber County.

## Federal 🟢
- **FHA** — 3.5% down, 580+ credit. 2026 single-family limit ~**$524,225** in Salt Lake,
  Utah, and Davis counties
- **VA** — 0% down, no PMI
- **USDA** — rural, 100% financing (relevant for outer Weber/Davis)

---

## Stacking

The highest-value conversation you can offer a first-time buyer is **stacking**: UHC
FirstHome + up-to-6%/$27,500 DPA, or SB 240's $20K layered onto an FHA 3.5%-down new build.
"You may be able to combine these" is a strong, compliant, non-demographic hook — it
describes programs, not people.
