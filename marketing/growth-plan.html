Growing a first-time-buyer practice inside the rules that govern housing ads

A Meta and Google operating plan built around the constraint most agents treat as an obstacle — and a few Utah-specific openings almost nobody is running.

Prepared Aug 2026 Market Wasatch Front Planning CPL $20–45 Regime Meta HEC + Google Housing

You cannot target first-time buyers. So stop trying — and put the qualifying in the creative instead.

Under the Housing category, Meta strips age, gender, ZIP, income, and every "likely to move" signal. Every competitor in your market faces the same stripped-down targeting. That flattens the advantage of whoever spends most on audience tooling and shifts it to whoever writes the sharpest offer. The agent with the most specific, most local, most dollar-denominated hook wins — and Utah hands you four of those for free.

Do not try to route around this

Meta's 2026 classifiers read your images (exteriors, floor plans, "For Sale" signage), your copy, your landing page, and your account history, and will auto-apply the Housing category whether or not you select it. Failing to declare is logged as an Evasion violation — strictly worse than declaring, and it degrades account health permanently. Declare it yourself, every time.

BTwo channels, two jobs

The regulations hit these platforms asymmetrically. That asymmetry should drive your budget split, not a 50/50 habit.

Meta · Facebook & Instagram

Demand creation

Targeting is gutted, so Meta's job is reaching people who aren't searching yet — the renter in Clearfield who has never run the numbers. Broad audience, sharp creative, program dollars in the hook.

Wins: seller valuation offers, DPA program awareness, renter-to-owner math, retargeting.

Loses: anything needing precision. Stop fighting it.

Google · Search

Intent capture

Keyword intent is not a protected signal, so the housing restriction barely touches Search. Someone typing "utah down payment assistance 2026" has self-identified more precisely than any Meta audience ever could.

Wins: program-name searches, "first time home buyer utah", city + price queries.

Loses: volume. The market is small; you'll cap out.

Recommended split at $2,000/mo

Google $700 — capture everything available; this pool is finite and high-converting. Meta $1,000 — split between seller valuation ($400) and buyer DPA ($600). Reserve $300 — retargeting once your pixel pool clears ~1,000 users. Expect roughly 45–100 leads/month at the $20–45 band. Below ~$20 CPL you are probably collecting low-intent form fills; check lead quality before celebrating.

Compliant copy that still qualifies hard

Seller · lowest CPL offer Layton homes are selling for more than most owners think. Get an instant estimate for your address — no calls unless you ask for one.

City named in copy, not in targeting. "No calls unless you ask" lifts form completion sharply.

Buyer · SB 240 Utah will put $20,000 toward your first home. 0% interest, no monthly payment, new construction under $450,000. Most people who qualify have never heard of it.

The dollar figure and the $450K cap do the demographic filtering that Meta won't let you do in the audience builder — legally, and better.

Buyer · Own in Ogden Ogden teachers: $15,000 toward a down payment. Ogden police and firefighters: $20,000. Everyone else inside city limits: $10,000. Here's how the program works.

Naming a profession in copy is not prohibited targeting — you're describing the program's own tiers. This is the single most precise compliant hook available to you.

Renter conversion Renting in Clearfield? The gap between your rent and a mortgage is probably smaller than the down payment standing in the way — and Utah has $20,000 for that. Run your own numbers in 90 seconds.

Describes the math and the program, never the person. Safe under HEC.

Verified: do not run "your rent already covers a mortgage"

It is the most common renter-conversion hook in real estate advertising and it is false in this market at current rates. A $340,000 home at 3.5% down and 6.5% carries roughly $2,485/month all-in (principal, interest, ~0.55% Utah property tax, insurance, FHA MIP) against typical Clearfield rent near $1,500–1,700. Payment would have to fall to about $214,000 in purchase price to break even against $1,600 rent — there is no such move-in inventory on the Wasatch Front.

Lead with the down payment as the obstacle instead. That framing is true, it is the actual barrier for first-time buyers, and it hands you the DPA programs as the answer.

Copy that will get you rejected

"Perfect for young families." "Great starter home for newlyweds." "Ideal for retirees." "Safe neighborhood for kids." Anything implying who should live somewhere is a Fair Housing problem, not just an ad-policy one. Describe the property and the program. Never the person.

DCounty plays

Your three counties have materially different programs. This is the local knowledge no national competitor bidding into your market has.

Weber · Ogden

Own in Ogden

$10–20K

0% deferred, repaid at sale. First-time status not required. Tiered: $10K general, $15K state-certified K-12 teachers and city employees, $20K sworn police and fire.

Price cap ~$430K existing / ~$467K new. Stack with the ~$5K Weber County CDBG grant (forgiven after 5 years) and FHLB HELP (~$20K forgivable).

Davis

County assistance

Up to $50K

1% loan, deferred, repaid at sale or refi. ≤80% AMI. Not restricted to first-time buyers — the largest single figure in your market.

2025 funds were exhausted. Applications were scheduled to reopen around July 1, 2026. Confirm live funding before any creative names this number.

Salt Lake

Fragmented, stackable

$10–15K

SLC HOME ~$10K · CDCU deferred forgivable (SLC / county / Taylorsville) · NeighborWorks ~$10K forgivable · West Valley City up to ~$14,999.

No single dominant program, so lead with SB 240 and UHC FirstHome here instead, and treat city programs as the closing detail.

Verify before you spend

Most county and city figures above come from lender and aggregator sites rather than primary county pages. Several programs cycle annually or exhaust mid-year. An exhausted program named in a live ad is a false-advertising exposure — confirm current funding with a participating lender before launching any creative containing a dollar amount, and pull the creative the day funding closes.

Also pending: HB 541 would extend SB 240–style help to existing homes (up to $10,000). Introduced, not confirmed passed. Do not advertise it yet. And UHC HomeAgain is currently suspended — route repeat buyers to FHA/VA or HFA Advantage.

The stack conversation

The highest-value thing you can say to a first-time buyer is that these combine. UHC FirstHome plus DPA up to 6% capped at $27,500, layered against SB 240's $20K on a new build, against an FHA 3.5%-down base. "You may be able to combine these" is a compliant, non-demographic hook that almost no competitor is making — and it's the reason a buyer picks you over the listing agent on the sign.

EThe network engine

Paid ads rent attention. These four relationships compound, and three of them feed the one audience lever HEC left you: your own first-party list.

The strongest play in this document

UHC FirstHome requires homebuyer education. That is a mandatory step every qualified Utah first-time buyer must complete before closing. Become the place they complete it. Co-host a monthly class with a UHC-participating loan officer and a title partner — you meet every serious FTHB in your counties before they choose an agent, at the exact moment they're choosing one. It is the cheapest, most defensible lead source available to you, it doubles as ad creative ("free homebuyer class, seats limited"), and every attendee legally enters your CRM — the one audience Meta still lets you target.

Partnership 01

UHC-participating lenders

Pick two or three loan officers who actively write FirstHome and SB 240. Co-brand the DPA explainer campaigns and split spend. They carry program authority you don't; you carry the audience.

Ask each for current funding status monthly — that's also your compliance check from §D.

Partnership 02

New-construction builders

SB 240 is new-construction-only. That single rule means builder relationships are the SB 240 channel — there is no other way to deliver that $20K.

Approach builders with inventory under the $450K cap in west Weber and southwest Salt Lake. You bring pre-qualified SB 240 buyers; they bring co-op marketing dollars.

Partnership 03

Property managers

Renters at lease-end are your buyer pipeline. Offer PMs a rent-vs-own class for tenants approaching renewal — they lose a tenant but keep goodwill, and you convert the ones who were leaving anyway.

Partnership 04

Past clients & sphere

Under HEC your CRM Custom Audience is one of the last precise levers standing. Upload it, keep it current, and run a low-budget always-on retargeting layer against it and its Special Ad Audience.

Every class attendee, every valuation request, every open-house signature belongs in it.

FCampaign build

Meta

Keep it deliberately simple — with targeting stripped there is nothing to gain from a sprawling account, and Meta needs volume per ad set to exit learning.

  • Campaign 1 — Seller valuation. Housing category declared. One ad set, 15mi radius on Layton or Bountiful, 18+, all genders, no interests. 4–6 creatives, city named in each.
  • Campaign 2 — Buyer DPA. One ad set per county (Weber / Davis / Salt Lake), each carrying that county's program hook from §D.
  • Campaign 3 — Retargeting. Site visitors + video 50%+ viewers + CRM list. Launch once the pool passes ~1,000.
  • Lead forms: three fields maximum. Every added field costs completion. Qualify on the follow-up call, not the form.

Google

  • Program-name campaign — highest intent, cheapest clicks, almost no competition: sb 240 utah, utah housing corporation down payment, own in ogden program, davis county down payment assistance.
  • Category campaign — first time home buyer utah, down payment assistance utah, how much house can i afford utah.
  • Local inventory — homes for sale layton ut under 400k and county variants.
  • Negatives from day one: jobs, salary, license, school, for rent, apartments, zillow, redfin, calculator. Real estate search terms bleed into careers and rentals constantly; this list is the difference between a $28 and a $60 lead.
  • Geo: county-level or privacy-safe radius. Never ZIP.

Speed to lead beats everything downstream

At a $20–45 CPL, response time is worth more than any optimization in this document. A lead contacted in five minutes converts multiples better than one contacted in an hour. Build the follow-up before you build the campaign — if you can't answer fast, don't turn the ads on yet.

IPre-launch checklist

Run this against every campaign before it goes live. Your utah-fthb-ads skill enforces the same list automatically.

  • Housing category explicitly declared
  • No age, gender, or ZIP targeting anywhere in the build
  • Radius is 15 miles or greater
  • No standard Lookalikes — Special Ad Audiences or first-party Custom Audiences only
  • No audience exclusions configured
  • Copy describes the property and the program, never the person
  • Every dollar amount named is confirmed currently funded
  • Landing page claim matches the ad claim exactly
  • Lead form is three fields or fewer
  • Follow-up process can respond within five minutes
  • Campaign stays paused until a human un-pauses it
